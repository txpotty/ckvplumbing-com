    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in South Burlington

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in South Burlington, VT

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in South Burlington, VT. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. Both kits ride on the truck. Genuine parts keep the warranty intact.

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in South Burlington

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in South Burlington, mostly in basements and utility closets. Their parts are simple, and a universal thermocouple fits nearly all of them. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. One goes in while we are there.

    Burner Tray and Venting Repair in South Burlington

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your South Burlington home.     Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in South Burlington, VT

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. Plenty of leaks come from the drain valve, the relief valve or a sweating supply nipple. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a South Burlington, VT home. If the bottom seam is wet, the tank has failed. Nothing patches that.

    Venting and Draft Repair in South Burlington, VT

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. We fix the vent. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.
