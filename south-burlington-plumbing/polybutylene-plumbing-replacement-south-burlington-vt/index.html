    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in South Burlington

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper runs higher, and a yard line usually adds $1,500 to $4,000.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Leaks at fittings are another. Since poly can look normal right before it splits, a home from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe should plan a replacement even before any leaks.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. Copper jobs use domestic Type L, and we keep one PEX brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    A full poly repipe in South Burlington, VT usually takes three to seven days. Size is the main factor. We give you a clear day-by-day timeline at the estimate, including which rooms we will work in each day and when the drywall finish will happen.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper often goes further. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Big houses need more time. The schedule depends on the number of fixtures, attic access and whether you choose PEX or copper, and water is usually back on at the end of each work day.

    A large share of Vermont homes rely on private wells, and well water here can range from soft to fairly hard depending on the bedrock. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.
