    Propane Gas Line Service in Shelburne, VT

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. Our work covers propane yard lines, regulator replacement and fitting repairs, plus a check that every appliance has its propane orifices installed. Wrong orifices make a burner run dirty and unsafe. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.

    Buried Yard Line Repair and Replacement in Shelburne, VT

    Many older homes have steel gas lines running from the meter to the house or out to a pool heater. The wrap fails eventually. After the coating breaks down, corrosion starts, and constant moisture from irrigation or poor drainage makes it worse. Utilities get marked first. We lay polyethylene with tracer wire or coated steel where exposed, then backfill and clean up after the test passes.

    After-Hours Gas Line Repair in Shelburne

    Permits and Code Compliance for Gas Work in Shelburne, VT

    Most gas piping work beyond a simple connector swap needs a permit in Shelburne, VT. The inspector checks pipe sizing, material, support spacing, shutoff locations, sediment traps and the pressure test result before signing off. We handle the paperwork. A permitted repair also gives you a paper trail for insurance and for any future sale.

    Gas Line Relocation and Upgrades in Shelburne, VT

    A kitchen remodel or a new tankless water heater can push a gas system past what its original pipe can deliver. Tankless units need a lot of gas. A whole-house tankless heater can draw 150,000 BTU or more, and that often means upsizing the gas line all the way back toward the meter. We calculate the full load first. The new pipe gets sized so every burner in the house still has full pressure at once.

    Gas Leak Detection in Shelburne

    A rotten egg smell, hissing near a line, dead grass in a stripe across the yard or a gas meter that moves with everything off are the common leak signs. Trust your nose. We isolate sections of the system and use electronic combustible gas detectors to narrow the source, then confirm with soap solution at fittings, valves and unions. After we locate the leak, we price the repair in writing.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Shelburne

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Rocky glacial soil and ledge are common across Vermont, which can make digging for water lines, sewer repairs or septic work slow and costly. Rusted buried steel gets replaced with polyethylene and tracer wire, and we use a listed transition riser where it rises to the house. Exposed sections get coated steel. We test before backfilling so any problem joint is fixed while the trench is still open.
