    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in St. Johnsbury

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. A slab home is often a little cheaper to plumb than a basement home, because basement drains may need an ejector pump and the layout uses more vertical pipe. We price gas piping as its own line. A square-foot guess misses too much, so we bid from plans.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in Vermont homes. It tolerates hot water and avoids the pinhole leaks copper can develop in some water. Drains are Schedule 40 PVC. Under slabs, we keep joints to a minimum and use long-sweep fittings at every change of direction so a cable or camera can get through later.

    Heating systems, boilers and water heaters work hard through the winter, so annual service helps prevent breakdowns in the coldest weeks. Frozen pipes are a common winter problem in Vermont, especially in older homes with plumbing in exterior walls or unheated spaces. Pipe routes and insulation get chosen to match. Often it means no supply lines in exterior walls, insulation on attic runs and frost-free hose bibs. Attics get the most attention.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. A late pour or a framing change can stack plumbing phases on top of each other. Plan changes after rough-in are the other big one, because moving a tub or adding a bar sink once walls are framed means re-drilling and new inspections. Every change gets priced in writing before we move pipe.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling fixtures or sewer odor point to venting or slope problems. A camera inspection through the cleanout will show a belly, an offset joint or debris left in the line, and it is worth doing before the builder's warranty period ends. Construction debris is common.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out follows framing at three to five days, and trim waits for cabinets and counters. Trim is one or two days.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine.
