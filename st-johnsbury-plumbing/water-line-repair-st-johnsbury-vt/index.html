    Buried Water Line Work in St. Johnsbury, VT

    Copper Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.

    PEX Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury, VT

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and pipe left in the sun before it was buried can get brittle. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in St. Johnsbury

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. Trenchless avoids most of that. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, following the old line or cutting a new path beside it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury, VT

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury, VT

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. CKV Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in St. Johnsbury

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in St. Johnsbury

    Underground leaks hide well. The first clue is often a water bill that jumped for no reason. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in St. Johnsbury

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Small repairs often do not. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. Polybutylene too, when we find it. For new work in St. Johnsbury, we usually recommend Type K copper or PEX rated for direct burial, or HDPE for a trenchless pull. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. A full replacement takes about a day. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull can stretch to two or three days, mostly because of restoration work and inspection timing. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. First, a cable is threaded through. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, while the new pipe is pulled into the path it just opened. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, which most residential service lines are.

    Yes. We warranty our labor. New pipe and fittings also carry the manufacturer warranty, and some PEX and HDPE makers back their pipe for 25 years or more. The written estimate spells out the coverage term before work begins, so there is no guessing later about what is included for our St. Johnsbury customers.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.
