    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in St. Johnsbury

    Labor to install a customer-supplied standard toilet typically runs $200 to $400. Including a mid-range toilet, most installs total $450 to $900, while wall-hung systems with carriers range from $1,500 to $3,500 or more. We give you a firm price up front.

    TOTO, Kohler and American Standard reliably perform well in flush tests and have good parts availability. Gerber and Mansfield are solid budget options. We help you match the brand and model to your needs.

    A large share of Vermont homes rely on private wells, and well water here can range from soft to moderately hard depending on the bedrock. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup in toilet tanks and bowls over time, affecting performance and requiring more regular cleaning. While it doesn't directly impact installation, choosing a toilet with a good glaze and strong flush can help reduce these effects.

    A standard toilet installation, assuming no major plumbing modifications are needed, typically takes about 1 to 3 hours. More complex installations, such as those requiring new rough-ins or dealing with damaged subflooring, can take longer.

    While it's possible for a homeowner to attempt a DIY toilet installation, it's usually best left to professionals. Improper installation can lead to leaks, wobbling, inefficient flushing, and potential water damage. Professional plumbers deliver proper sealing, secure mounting, and adherence to codes.

    A one-piece toilet has the bowl and tank integrated into a single unit, offering a sleek look and easier cleaning. A two-piece toilet has a separate bowl and tank, which are bolted together during installation. Both types are widely available and perform well.

    Toilets carry manufacturer warranties, often 1 to 5 years on parts and longer on porcelain. We also guarantee our installation workmanship and will come back to fix any leak caused by the install.

    The rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, typically 12 inches but sometimes 10 or 14. Buying a toilet with the wrong rough-in means it will not fit or will sit too far from the wall.
