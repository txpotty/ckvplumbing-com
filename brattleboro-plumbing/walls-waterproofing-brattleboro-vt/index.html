    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Brattleboro, VT

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Vermont has some of the oldest housing in the country, including farmhouses and village homes built well over a century ago. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Brattleboro, VT

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, a drop of about six inches in the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. Every option comes written out with its price.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Brattleboro

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Brattleboro

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Most Vermont homes have full basements, many with fieldstone or older foundations that can let in groundwater during wet seasons. Backfill goes in last.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Brattleboro

    Vermont has some of the oldest housing in the country, including farmhouses and village homes built well over a century ago. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and add drain tile wherever water collects at the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Brattleboro

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Short summers with occasional humidity bring condensation on cold pipes in basements, which can cause drips and mold if left alone. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Brattleboro

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Most vertical cracks get sealed in one visit.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Brattleboro, VT

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Brattleboro

    Most jobs fall between $3,000 for interior crack sealing and $15,000 or more for exterior excavation with new footing drains. The size of the wall, how wet it gets, the method we pick and how easy the wall is to reach all move the number. Access matters most. You get a written quote before any work starts.

    It depends on the wall and the site. On exterior foundation work we use liquid polyurethane-modified asphalt, rubberized asphalt sheets, bentonite panels and crystalline coatings, picking the one that suits the soil and the wall material. For showers, we use Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, generally more than about ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. Small patches we cut out and replace ourselves.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, so the studs and sheathing rot faster than if the wall had been left alone. We repair the leak and dry the cavity first.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide because soil movement, the water table and the quality of the original install all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Drain tile can silt up over time.

    Check the basement after a heavy rain. Wet spots on the walls, mold or mildew, a musty smell in the crawl space, new foundation cracks, efflorescence on the concrete or standing water on the floor all mean water is getting in somewhere. Those signs usually get worse, not better.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on weather and how deep the footing sits. Your timeline comes with the quote.
