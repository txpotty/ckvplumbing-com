    Shower Repairs We Handle in Rutland, VT

    Emergency Shower Repair in Rutland, VT

    A shower that will not shut off is an emergency. Shut the main, or use the shower's integral stops if your valve has them behind the trim or an access panel. Then call us. CKV Plumbing sends a plumber with cartridges on the truck, and we can often get your water back on within the hour.

    Moen and Delta Shower Repair in Rutland, VT

    Moen 1222 and Delta RP46074 cartridges sit inside a huge share of the showers we open. The Moen 1222 runs the Posi-Temp line, and the Delta RP46074 runs the Monitor 13 and 14 series, and both wear out on a schedule you can almost set a calendar by. Both ride on our trucks. Many of these carry a lifetime warranty to the original owner.

    Shower Valve Replacement in Rutland

    Temperature that swings or a handle that fights you points to the valve. When the body is corroded or the brand stopped making cartridges for it, we replace the valve, usually through a small access panel behind the shower wall so the tile stays put. The new valve will have scald protection. You can step up to a thermostatic valve for tighter control.

    Same-Day Shower Leak Repair in Rutland, VT

    Shut off the main first if water is getting where it should not. For a sudden wall leak or a valve that will not close, the main valve near the meter is the first move. Then call us. Our plumber stops the water, fixes it or makes it safe and checks for wet areas.

    Dripping Showerhead Repair in Rutland

    Drips from the head itself almost always come from the valve, not the head. If the head drips with the valve closed, water is slipping past the cartridge, and a new head will not change that. The fix is in the valve. Leaks at the arm threads are different, and those get new thread tape and a snug turn.

    Leaking Shower Cartridge Repair in Rutland

    Cartridges wear from the inside. Mineral grit scores the sealing faces and flattens the O-rings until the cartridge can no longer stop the flow. A new cartridge fixes it. The new cartridge goes in with silicone grease, which keeps the handle smooth.

    Hot Water Pressure Problems in Rutland, VT

    Weak hot water with strong cold usually rules out the showerhead. The problem is on the hot side, either sediment in the water heater outlet, a partly closed valve at the heater or a cartridge whose hot port is packed with scale. Each side gets tested. If the sinks have good hot pressure and the shower does not, the cartridge is the first suspect.

    Showerhead and Arm Leak Repair in Rutland

    Shower arms leak at the threads more than people realize. Water that seeps from the arm connection runs back along the pipe into the wall, and you only find out when the paint on the other side starts to bubble. We reseal the threads. We can add a handheld or slide bar while we are there.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Shower Repair in Rutland

    Most shower repairs, like a cartridge or diverter, cost $150 to $375. Valve replacements that require opening the wall usually run $450 to $1,200, depending on the valve and how much wall has to come open. Pan repairs cost more.

    Loose valve connections and failed grout cause most of them. Cracked pans, worn O-rings behind the trim and a loose shower arm connection can all let water into the wall cavity without showing on the tile side. We find it with a meter. The repair uses the smallest opening we can manage.

    Unscrew the head and soak it in vinegar first. Still weak? The cartridge or restrictor may need work.

    We repair Moen, Delta, Kohler, Pfister, American Standard, Symmons and many others. Common cartridges ride on our trucks.

    Plan on a morning at most for the common ones. A cartridge or clog is about an hour, while a valve swap can run half a day. We keep the bathroom usable.

    Worn cartridge or seals, almost always. Hard water speeds that wear, which is why drips are so common, and a new cartridge with fresh silicone grease usually stops it for years.

    Watch for drips and temperature swings first. Low pressure, standing water at the drain, banging pipes when the handle closes and stains on the ceiling below all point to a problem worth a look. Catching it early saves money.
