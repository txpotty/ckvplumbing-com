    Clogged Grease Trap Repair in Burlington

    Hardened grease in the inlet tee is the usual culprit when a trap backs up. The fix starts with pumping the trap down and jetting the inlet line until it flows again. Food solids packed against the baffle get scraped out by hand. Then we look for the reason it clogged, such as a dishwasher dumping too much hot water at once or a missing sink strainer upstream. A clog that keeps coming back nearly always has a cause you can see.

    Commercial Grease Trap Repair in Burlington

    Restaurants, school cafeterias, hospital kitchens and bakeries all push more grease than a small trap can hold. We see undersized traps fail in the same few places every time. The inlet tee clogs, the outlet tee gets coated, and grease starts showing up in the sewer manhole down the street, which is usually how the city finds out before the owner does. We fix the failed parts and tell you plainly if the trap is too small for the kitchen.

    Preventative Grease Trap Maintenance & Repair in Burlington, VT

    Most trap repairs we do trace back to pumping intervals that stretched too long. Pretreatment programs commonly set the limit at 25 percent of the liquid depth for grease and solids combined. After a repair, we measure the grease cap and solids with a sludge judge and suggest a pumping schedule based on how fast your trap actually fills. Busy fryers fill a trap fast.

    Inlet and Outlet Tee Repair in Burlington, VT

    Tees are cheap parts with an outsized job. On the inlet side, the tee directs incoming water down under the floating grease. The outlet tee draws water from low in the tank, well under the grease cap, so if it breaks off or someone removes it during pumping, floating grease walks right out to the sewer. We replace broken tees in Schedule 40 PVC and set the drop to the depth the code or the manufacturer calls for.

    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Trap Repair in Burlington

    A cable machine punches a hole through grease. A jetter scrubs the whole pipe wall instead. We run a commercial jetter at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI through the line feeding the trap and the line leaving it, working from the cleanout toward the main. With old cast iron, we turn the pressure down and keep a camera on it, since a thin wall can blow out.

    Cost-Effective Grease Trap Solutions in Burlington, VT

    Pricing depends on what failed, so we quote after the trap is pumped and we can see inside it. Replacing a gasket or a tee is a modest bill.     Damaged Baffle Repair in Burlington

    Baffles are the walls inside the trap that slow the water down long enough for grease to float and solids to sink. Once one cracks or falls loose, the trap is just a box that grease passes through. We pump it out and pull the old baffle. Plastic units get a factory replacement part. Steel ones often need a fabricated baffle or a new trap.

    Leaking Grease Trap Repair in Burlington, VT

    A wet floor around an under-sink trap usually points to a failed lid gasket or a loose inlet coupling. Steel trap bodies also rust through at the bottom seams after enough years of hot, acidic water. We dry the area, fill the trap and watch where the water shows up. Some days the fix is a cheap gasket and ten minutes. If the seam itself has opened, patching rarely holds and we will tell you so before you pay for it.
