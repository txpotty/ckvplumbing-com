    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Burlington

    Most toilet replacements, including a quality standard toilet, labor and disposal, cost $450 to $950. Premium one-piece or smart toilets can raise the total to $1,200 to $2,500. Flange repairs, if needed, usually add $100 to $250.

    Manufacturers typically warranty parts for 1 to 5 years and porcelain longer. We guarantee our workmanship and will return if a leak is caused by the installation.

    It is possible for handy homeowners, but old toilets are heavy and flange problems are common once the toilet is pulled. A pro replacement prevents cracked porcelain, leaks and damaged floors.

    A large share of Vermont homes rely on private wells, and well water here can range from soft to fairly hard depending on the bedrock. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup in toilet tanks and bowls, affecting flush mechanisms and causing stains. When replacing a toilet, we can recommend models with surfaces designed to fight mineral buildup and discuss water softening options.

    The rough-in measurement refers to the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drainpipe (the bolts that secure the toilet). Standard rough-ins are typically 12 inches, but 10-inch and 14-inch options exist. Knowing your rough-in is essential to make sure your new toilet fits correctly in the existing space.

    Signs of failing internal toilet parts include a toilet that runs constantly, a weak or incomplete flush, a handle that needs jiggling, or visible wear and tear on components like the flapper, fill valve, or flush valve. While some parts can be replaced individually, an older toilet with multiple failing components might benefit more from a full replacement.

    Older toilets can use 3.5 to 7 gallons per flush, while WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons or less. For a typical household, that can mean thousands of gallons saved each year.
