    Float Switch Inspection and Adjustment in Burlington, VT

    The float switch is a critical component that tells your sump pump when to turn on and off. A stuck or improperly adjusted float switch can lead to the pump running continuously or failing to activate at all. During maintenance, we inspect the float switch for freedom of movement and proper calibration, making adjustments as needed to ensure reliable operation for your home in Burlington, VT.

    Pit Cleaning and Debris Removal in Burlington

    Long dry stretches let dust, sand, gravel and leaves settle in the pit. That debris can get sucked into the intake screen and jam the impeller. We pump down and scoop out the basin, rinse the intake and inspect the pit liner for cracks. A clean basin helps the pump run cooler and last years longer.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Burlington, VT

    Many Burlington, VT homeowners rely on battery backup sump pumps for protection during power outages. CKV Plumbing provides essential maintenance for these critical systems, including testing the battery's charge and health, inspecting the backup pump's operation and checking the charging system. This keeps your home protected even when the primary power source fails, a common concern during severe storms.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Burlington, VT

    The float switch is the most frequent reason a sump pump fails to start. Tethered floats can tangle on the pump body, and vertical floats can stick from mineral crust or debris. We clean or replace the switch and set the on and off levels so the pump does not short cycle. Electronic sensor switches are an option for tight pits where floats get hung up.

    Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Burlington

    Ice dams and frozen ground in late winter can send meltwater toward foundations, making a working sump pump important. We test your pump ahead of the rainy season by filling the pit until the float trips. We watch the pump empty the basin, listen for grinding bearings and measure how fast it cycles. If the pump struggles, you still have time to fix it before the first big storm.
