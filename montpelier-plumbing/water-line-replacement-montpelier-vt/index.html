    Burst Main Line Response in Montpelier, VT

    Burst lines do damage fast. We get the water off, dig to the break, and decide on site whether a clamp or a short section of new pipe will hold until a full replacement can be scheduled, or whether the line is too far gone to bother patching. You will not be without water overnight if we can help it.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Montpelier, VT

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Copper and poly are better pulled than burst. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, leaving you with two small holes instead of a long trench. Rock can stop it.

    Traditional Water Line Excavation in Montpelier

    Sometimes the open trench wins. If the old pipe has collapsed or turns hard corners, we dig. We cut the sod and set it aside, dig to depth with a mini excavator or by hand near other utilities, lay the new pipe on a sand bed, and backfill in layers with compaction so the trench does not sink after the first good rain. Sod goes back on top.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Montpelier

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so the full line costs less than a string of repairs. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Montpelier

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Montpelier, VT

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside when exposed to chlorinated water. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some home insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Worth replacing.

    Emergency Water Line Replacement in Montpelier

    Water bubbling up through the lawn or the street side of the sidewalk means a break, not a drip. First step, shut off the main valve. If that does not stop it, the break is on your side of the meter but before the valve, and we will close the curb stop with a meter key and plan the replacement from there, usually starting the same or next day.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Montpelier

    PEX comes in coils up to several hundred feet. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, because one coil covers the whole run without a joint. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.
