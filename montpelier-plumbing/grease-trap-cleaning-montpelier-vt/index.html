    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Montpelier

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. The waste still needs legal disposal. A common setup is daily skimming by staff and a full pump and scrape from us every few weeks, so the records stay complete.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Plan one to three hours for an outdoor interceptor. The long end is for big tanks with a hard grease cap, where the crew has to break up the cap with a pressure wand before the suction hose can move it.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Monthly pumping is common for busy restaurants. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Most people use trap for the small indoor unit, usually under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.
