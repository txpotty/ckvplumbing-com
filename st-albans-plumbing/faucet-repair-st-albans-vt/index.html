    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in St. Albans

    Most faucet repairs cost $125 to $300, including the cartridge or seals and labor. Complex kitchen or touchless faucet repairs may run $200 to $400. We quote a flat price before any work starts.

    Faucet repair involves fixing the existing fixture by replacing specific worn or broken internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Faucet replacement means removing the old faucet entirely and installing a brand new one. We recommend replacement when a faucet is badly damaged, very old, or if you simply want an upgrade.

    Our plumbers are skilled at repairing all major faucet brands commonly found in Vermont homes, including Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard, Pfister, and many others. We keep a stock of universal and common brand-specific parts to speed up repairs.

    For a standard faucet repair, where only internal components are replaced and the fixture itself remains, no plumbing permits are usually required in St. Albans. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or installing a new type of fixture that requires new connections, a permit might be necessary. We will advise you if any permits are needed for your specific situation.

    While some minor leaks, like a simple washer replacement on a compression faucet, can be a DIY project, most modern faucets involve specific tools and intricate parts like cartridges or ceramic discs. Incorrect repairs can lead to further damage, more significant leaks, or voided warranties. It's often best to call a professional for a proper and lasting fix.

    Town water systems in St. Albans and other communities are generally reliable, but older service lines can add rust and discolored water. Hard water contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or timely repair important.

    Low pressure at one faucet is typically a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure everywhere could mean a failing pressure regulator, a supply issue or old corroded pipes.

    While a minor drip isn't usually an immediate emergency, it's a problem that should be addressed quickly to prevent water waste and potential damage. However, a rapidly gushing leak or a faucet that won't shut off completely is an emergency, requiring immediate professional attention to avoid significant water damage.
