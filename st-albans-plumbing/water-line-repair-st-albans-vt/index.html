    Copper Water Line Repair in St. Albans

    Copper pipes are known for their durability and longevity, but they can still develop leaks due to corrosion, excessive water pressure, or physical damage.     Emergency Water Line Break Repair in St. Albans, VT

    A full break can flood a yard, wash out soil under walkways and leave your home without water.     Main Water Line Leak Repair in St. Albans

    Most main line leaks show up as a soggy strip of yard, a sudden jump in your water bill or a meter dial that moves with every fixture off. We verify the leak by isolating the house shutoff and watching the meter. Then we use ground microphones to find the exact spot. A single section is cut out and replaced with repair couplings or a new length of pipe, and the line is pressure tested before backfill.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in St. Albans, VT

    Leaks often start at the compression fittings and couplings right next to the meter box. Shifting soil and heavy traffic over the box can loosen those joints over time. We determine whether the leak is on the utility side or the customer side, since the water provider is responsible for the meter and the pipe before it. When the leak is yours, we replace the fittings and the customer shutoff valve on the same trip.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in St. Albans

    Slab leaks, where water lines running beneath your home's concrete foundation leak, are a particularly challenging issue. Vermont has some of the oldest housing in the country, including farmhouses and village homes built well over a century ago. These leaks can undermine your foundation, leading to serious structural damage. We use specialized listening devices and camera inspections to pinpoint slab leaks precisely and offer repair options including direct slab penetration, re-routing, or pipe lining. Addressing a slab leak quickly in St. Albans is crucial to prevent extensive, costly foundation damage.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in St. Albans, VT

    Many homes built before the 1970s still have galvanized steel service lines that rust from the inside. Town water systems in St. Albans and other communities are generally reliable, but older service lines can add sediment and discolored water.
