    Underground and Under-Slab Plumbing in St. Albans

    Underground work sets the drain, waste and vent layout that every fixture in the house relies on. Most Vermont homes have full basements, many with fieldstone or older foundations that can let in groundwater during wet seasons. We trench, bed the pipe, and set stubs to the form boards so they come up exactly where the plans show. Two inches off puts a drain under a plate. The whole underground holds a water test with a standpipe before any concrete goes in.

    Water Heater Setup for New Builds in St. Albans

    Tank or tankless, the venting decides the install location. A power-vent tank can run PVC out a sidewall, while an atmospheric tank needs a metal flue that goes up through the roof. We set the heater on a stand in a garage where code requires it. Every one gets a T&P line piped to an approved spot.

    Fixture Installation and Trim-Out in St. Albans, VT

    After cabinets and countertops are in, we come back for trim-out. We set toilets on fresh rings, hook up faucets with braided supplies and check every angle stop for drips. Then we run water everywhere. A slow drain at trim usually means debris in the line, so we clear it before the final inspection rather than leaving it for the homeowner.

    Final Trim and Fixture Setting in St. Albans

    At trim we install toilets, faucets, garbage disposals and dishwashers, plus ice maker boxes and laundry connections. We let every fixture run long enough to show a slow drip. We also check that hot and cold are on the correct sides, set the water heater to 120 degrees, and clean aerators clogged by construction grit. Then the walkthrough.

    Residential New Construction Plumbing in St. Albans

    Layout drives cost on a house. Grouping the kitchen, laundry and baths along one wet wall can cut a few hundred feet of pipe out of a plan. Plan review is where we flag them. Changing a bath location on paper costs nothing, while moving it after the slab is poured means a saw cut and a new drain trench.
