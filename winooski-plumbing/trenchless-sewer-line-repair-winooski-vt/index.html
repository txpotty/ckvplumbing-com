    Sectional Point Repair in Winooski

    When only one section of a line is damaged, a point repair can fix it without lining the whole length. We position a short resin liner at the damaged spot using the camera and inflate it until it cures. Point repairs are a cost-effective fix for a single crack, hole or root entry. They are common on otherwise healthy lines.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Winooski, VT

    CIPP relining creates a new pipe within an existing damaged sewer line. A resin-saturated felt tube is inserted into the old pipe and then inflated, curing in place to form a smooth, durable and watertight liner. This technique is well suited for repairing cracks, leaks and minor offsets without needing to dig up the entire line. It is a fast and effective solution for many common sewer problems faced by Vermont homeowners.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Winooski

    Some older homes still have Orangeburg fiber pipe or vitrified clay sewer laterals. Orangeburg flattens and blisters over time, while clay cracks and separates at joints. Pipe bursting is often the best option for Orangeburg, and lining can work well for clay that still holds its shape. We determine the material on camera before recommending a method.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Winooski, VT

    Tree and shrub roots go after moisture in sewer joints. Once inside, they grow into dense masses that cause repeat backups. We cut roots with a mechanical cutter or hydro jet, then line the pipe to seal the joints. That breaks the cycle of cleaning the line every few months.

    Perma-Liner Trenchless Solutions in Winooski

    We use proven trenchless products like those from Perma-Liner, a leader in CIPP technology. Their materials and systems deliver a durable, long-lasting repair that stands up to the demands of local soil and climate conditions. Using trusted brands like Perma-Liner protects the integrity and longevity of our work.

    Pipe Bursting in Winooski, VT

    Pipe bursting replaces a badly damaged line by pulling a bursting head through the old pipe. The head breaks the old pipe outward while new HDPE pipe follows behind it. Bursting is best for collapsed or severely offset lines that cannot be lined. It usually requires an entry pit and an exit pit.
