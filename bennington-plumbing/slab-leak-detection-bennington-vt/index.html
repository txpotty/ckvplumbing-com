    Supply and Drain Leaks Under the Slab in Bennington, VT

    A supply leak and a drain leak show different signs. Supply leaks are pressurized, so they move the meter, warm the floor on the hot side and run whether or not anyone uses water. Drain leaks only leak when water goes down, and they show up as odors, slow drains or damp floors near bathrooms. Each needs its own test in your Bennington, VT home.

    Repiping After Repeat Slab Leaks in Bennington, VT

    Two slab leaks usually means more are coming. The rest of the pipe has likely aged the same way, so repiping replaces every supply line with new PEX or copper through the walls and attic, which ends the pattern of new leaks and new floor openings. A full repipe takes about two to four days.

    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Bennington

    Pressure isolation is always the first step. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which one drops over fifteen to thirty minutes. The gauge points us to the right line. It also rules out a toilet or faucet leak masquerading as a slab leak in your Bennington, VT home.

    Post-Tension Slab Leak Detection in Bennington

    A lot of newer houses are built on post-tension slabs. These slabs have steel tendons stretched under tens of thousands of pounds of force, and cutting one by mistake is dangerous and can damage the foundation. We scan for tendons with ground-penetrating radar or a rebar locator before opening anything, or recommend a reroute that never touches the concrete.

    Thermal Scans on Large Floors in Bennington, VT

    Big open floor plans hide leaks well. A thermal camera scans an entire living area in a few minutes, showing temperature differences of a degree or less across tile or concrete, which lets us rule out large areas before acoustic work starts. It saves an hour on large homes across Bennington, VT.
