    A one-piece toilet has the bowl and tank integrated into a single unit, offering a sleek look and easier cleaning. A two-piece toilet has a separate bowl and tank, which are bolted together during installation. Both types are widely available and perform well.

    When choosing a new toilet, consider factors like bowl shape (round vs. elongated), height (standard vs. ADA-compliant), flushing system (gravity-fed vs. pressure-assisted), water efficiency (gallons per flush), and overall style to match your bathroom. We can help you sort through these choices.

    The rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, typically 12 inches but sometimes 10 or 14. Buying a toilet with the wrong rough-in means it will not fit or will sit too far from the wall.

    A comfort height toilet has a bowl that is taller than standard models, typically around 17 to 19 inches from the floor to the top of the seat. This height is closer to a standard chair, making it more comfortable for many adults, especially those with mobility issues.

    Common signs that you might need a new toilet include frequent clogs, constant running, visible cracks in the bowl or tank, excessive wobbling, or a toilet that is very old and inefficient. If you're always repairing it, replacement is often more cost-effective.
