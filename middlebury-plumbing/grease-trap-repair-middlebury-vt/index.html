    Damaged Baffle Repair in Middlebury

    Baffles are the walls inside the trap that slow the water down long enough for grease to float and solids to sink. Once one cracks or falls loose, the trap is just a box that grease passes through. We pump it out and pull the old baffle. Plastic units get a factory replacement part. Steel traps with a rusted-out baffle may need a fabricated piece, and sometimes a new trap is the cheaper answer.

    Damaged Baffle and Lid Repair in Middlebury, VT

    Lids crack when someone sets a stockpot on them or a dolly rolls across a floor-mounted unit. A cracked lid lets odor out and lets mop water in. We replace the lid and gasket with the manufacturer part, and on in-floor units we check that the lid sits flush so nobody catches a toe or a cart wheel on it. Baffle damage often shows up at the same visit.

    Commercial Grease Trap Repair in Middlebury

    Restaurants, school cafeterias, hospital kitchens and bakeries all push more grease than a small trap can hold. Undersized traps fail in predictable spots. The inlet tee clogs, the outlet tee gets coated, and grease starts showing up in the sewer manhole down the street, which is usually how the city finds out before the owner does. We repair what broke, then tell you straight whether the unit is sized for your volume.

    Baffle Repair and Replacement in Middlebury

    Inside a concrete gravity interceptor the baffle is usually a poured or precast wall with a crossover opening. When that wall spalls, grease slips through to the second compartment. We can rebuild the wall with form work and a sulfate-resistant mix or set a polyethylene baffle panel, depending on the tank. Both require a full pump-out and a dry tank.

    Grease Trap Repair vs Replacement Cost in Middlebury, VT

    Age and material decide most repair versus replace calls. A 25-year-old steel trap with rust holes is done. A plastic or fiberglass trap with a broken baffle is usually worth fixing, and a concrete interceptor with sound walls and a bad riser almost always is, since replacing a buried tank means excavation and several days closed. You get repair and replacement priced side by side, in writing.

    Preventative Grease Trap Maintenance & Repair in Middlebury, VT

    Most trap repairs we do trace back to pumping intervals that stretched too long. The common rule is to pump when grease and solids fill a quarter of the liquid depth. After a repair, we measure the grease cap and solids with a sludge judge and suggest a pumping schedule based on how fast your trap actually fills. Busy fryers fill a trap fast.

    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Trap Repair in Middlebury

    A cable machine punches a hole through grease. A jetter scrubs the whole pipe wall instead. We run a commercial jetter at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI through the line feeding the trap and the line leaving it, working from the cleanout toward the main. With old cast iron, we turn the pressure down and keep a camera on it, since a thin wall can blow out.

    Grease Interceptor Clog Removal in Middlebury

    Grease does not stay liquid for long once it cools in a pipe. Within a few feet of the sink it hardens like candle wax along the top of the pipe. Chemical drain openers soften it for a day and then it re-forms farther downstream, sometimes past the trap entirely, where it becomes a sewer problem instead of a kitchen problem. Jetting removes it.
