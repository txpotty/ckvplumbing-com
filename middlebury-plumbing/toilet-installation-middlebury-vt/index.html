    New tile over old vinyl can raise a bathroom floor by half an inch or more. Suddenly the old flange is below the floor.     Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Middlebury

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. An access plate hides the tank. Because the carrier carries all the weight, we anchor its feet to the slab or framing first and check it for plumb before the drywall or tile goes up. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Rough-In Toilet Installation in Middlebury, VT

    Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close up. On a toilet, that is a vented 3 inch drain and a cold water stub. The drain goes 12 inches out from the finished wall line, which means we add the drywall thickness to the framing measurement. Then the inspector sees it open.

    Toilet Flange and Wax Ring Replacement in Middlebury, VT

    The closet flange is the ring that ties the toilet to the drain, and it is the weak point on most old installs. Cast iron flanges rust through at the bolt slots. PVC and ABS flanges crack when someone overtightens the bolts, and a repair ring screwed over the top is often all it takes to restore a solid anchor. Seal goes on last.

    Commercial Flushometer Toilets in Middlebury

    Sloan and Zurn flushometer valves are the standard on commercial toilets for a reason. There is no tank to refill.     Plan on one to three hours for a straight swap. Setting the bowl itself is the quick part. Add a new flange or a subfloor patch and it can run to half a day, which we will flag as soon as we see it.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. Some older homes and tight bathrooms have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet built for the wrong distance either will not fit against the wall or leaves an awkward gap behind the tank. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    Watch the base and the tank. Water that reappears on the floor after you wipe it up points to a cracked bowl or a failed seal. A seal can be replaced for far less than a new toilet, so we check that first, but a crack in the china means replacement, since there is no lasting repair for cracked porcelain.
