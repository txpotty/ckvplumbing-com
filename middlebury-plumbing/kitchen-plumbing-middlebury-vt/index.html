    Sinks, Disposals and Drains in Middlebury, VT: How We Handle Them

    Dishwasher and Ice Maker Lines in Middlebury

    A dishwasher needs a hot supply with its own shutoff, a drain to the disposal or a wye tailpiece, and an air gap on the sink deck or a high loop fastened under the counter, depending on local code. Ice makers need a cold line. We run braided stainless or copper to the fridge with a quarter-turn shutoff, never the thin plastic tubing that splits behind the fridge.

    Disposal Repair and Replacement in Middlebury, VT

    A disposal that hums but will not spin is almost always jammed. We cut the power, work the flywheel loose with a 1/4 inch hex key from underneath, pull out the bottle cap or bone, and press the red reset button before we run it again. Leaking units are another matter. A drip from the bottom of the housing means the internal seal failed, and that unit has to be replaced.

    InSinkErator Installation in Middlebury, VT

    Many homeowners ask to step up from an older InSinkErator to an Evolution because the newer units are quieter and grind finer. Often the mount stays. If the existing mount is the same Quick Lock style and in good shape, the swap goes faster and the sink flange does not have to come out, and we register the warranty for you.

    Grease Trap Installation and Cleaning in Middlebury

    Grease traps are sized by flow, and a three-compartment sink usually needs at least a 20 gallon per minute unit. We set the trap level, install the flow control fitting and vent it so it does not siphon. Cleaning schedules depend on volume. Busy fryer kitchens may need it every few weeks.

    Burst Supply Lines in Middlebury, VT

    Plastic supply lines with plastic nuts crack more often than braided stainless, especially after years of pressure swings. A failed line under the sink can put gallons per minute into the cabinet and the floor below before anyone notices it. We replace both sides. If one line cracked from age, the other one is about the same age.

    Grease Trap Installation in Middlebury, VT

    Under-sink grease traps work for small kitchens, while full restaurants often need an in-ground interceptor sized to the local sewer authority's rules. The local rule gets checked before we price it. Trap size comes from fixture flow rates, and a trap that is too small fills quickly and lets grease slip into the sewer.
