    Why Choose CKV Plumbing for Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Middlebury, VT?

    A few hours below freezing is enough to split a small exposed pipe. Ice takes up roughly nine percent more room than water. In a closed pipe that expansion builds pressure between the ice plug and the faucet, often enough to split copper or crack a PVC fitting, and the leak only shows up once the ice melts.

    • Crack Checks, Every thawed line is pressure tested so a hidden split does not flood the house later.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Middlebury

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Middlebury usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Freeze-proofing is cheaper. Insulation, heat cable and frost-free faucets usually cost less than one burst repair.

    Most homes have water back within one to two hours of our arrival. A split pipe adds time. We isolate a split branch when we can, so the rest of the house has water while we repair it.

    Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawlspaces and garages, and seal air leaks around them. Disconnect garden hoses before cold weather. On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and keep the thermostat at the same setting overnight instead of turning it down.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Fixing a burst usually takes a few hours. Repairs inside finished ceilings or walls take longer because we open the drywall, repair the pipe, test it and leave the opening clean and ready for a patch, and multiple splits on one run add time as well.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Hidden lines take longer. A frozen line inside an exterior wall or under a floor can take a few hours, especially if it is PEX or CPVC, which we thaw with gentler infrared or warm air instead of an electric thawing machine.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Manufacturer warranties cover parts. It covers the repair, not a future freeze, so we recommend protection for exposed lines.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. Water drains out of the stem after shutoff. Leaving a hose attached defeats it, since trapped water can split the valve inside the wall.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Frost on exposed pipe is another. You may also notice a toilet that will not refill, a bulge in a pipe, or a strange smell from a drain, and any of those means you should open the faucet and call before the line thaws on its own.
