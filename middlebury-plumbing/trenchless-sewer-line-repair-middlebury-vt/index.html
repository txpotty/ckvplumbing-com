    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Middlebury

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Middlebury, VT

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints separate, crack or drop out of grade over the years. Most Vermont homes have full basements, many with fieldstone or older foundations that can let in water during wet seasons. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Age does the rest. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.

    Pipe Bursting in Middlebury, VT

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Middlebury, VT

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Lining in Middlebury, VT

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom. Lining it avoids cutting the floor. We coordinate around tenant hours. A typical commercial lining job runs one to three days, depending on footage and how many branch connections need to be reinstated with a cutter.

    A sudden collapse usually shows up as every drain in the house backing up at once, often into the lowest tub or floor drain. The first job is getting flow back, usually with a cable or a jetter.
